What moves your price

  • Size. More square footage of deck, or a longer run of fence, means more hours and more stain.
  • Condition. Gray, dry wood and flaking old finish need more cleaning and sanding than wood that has been kept up.
  • Repairs. Split, soft, or rotten boards are swapped before staining, and that work is quoted on top.
  • Stain type. Solid, semi-transparent, and clear stains differ in price and in how often they need redoing.
  • Coats. A deck takes more wear than a fence, so it usually gets more coats.

Prep and Repairs Done Before Staining

Stain only holds on clean, dry, sound wood, so we handle the prep that decides how long the finish lasts and tell you when a job needs a carpenter instead.

Washing and drying

Every job starts with a wash that lifts dirt, mildew, and what is left of the old finish, because new stain will not hold on top of grime. Then the wood dries all the way through, since stain over damp wood does not soak in.

Board replacement

Boards that are split through or soft when pressed get replaced before staining, since stain on rotten wood fails quickly. We handle board swaps that belong to a staining job, and a full deck rebuild is a carpenter's work.

Fasteners and splinters

Loose nails and screws get tightened or reset, and rough spots get sanded smooth, so the finished surface is safe to walk on barefoot.

Protecting what is around it

Plants, siding, and furniture get covered or moved before staining starts, so overspray and drips stay on the wood and off your house.

When You Need Deck Staining or Fence Staining

Bare wood tells you when the finish is gone, and waiting dries the wood out deeper, which makes the job bigger. Look for these signs:

  • Gray or silvery wood. The color has bleached out and nothing protects the fibers.
  • Water soaks in. Sprinkle a little on the boards. If it darkens the wood instead of beading up, the finish has worn off.
  • Rough or splintering spots. You feel them underfoot, usually first on stairs.
  • Thin stain at high-touch spots. Stair edges, railings, and gates wear first.
  • Dark patches. Mildew and moisture are settling into the grain.

Why Deck and Fence Wood Fails

Unprotected wood takes a beating from three directions at once, and most early stain failures come down to prep.

  • Sun. Ultraviolet light breaks down the surface fibers and bleaches out the color.
  • Moisture. Water that gets in swells the grain and eventually leads to rot, especially on horizontal boards.
  • Foot traffic. It grinds the finish away unevenly, fastest on stairs and around gates.
  • Skipped cleaning. Stain brushed over dirt or old flaking product does not bond, and it wears off in months.
  • Staining damp wood. Stain cannot soak into wet fibers, so it stays on the surface and peels.

Left alone, the surface roughens and boards start to cup and split, which turns a restain into a repair.

Deck Staining and Fence Staining Questions, Answered

How much does deck staining cost?

Homewyse's May 2026 national estimate is $2.96 to $6.16 per square foot for a deck with two coats of oil-based semi-transparent stain. Repairs to existing boards are not included, and neither are local differences, so we put your total in writing before work starts.

How much does fence staining cost?

Homewyse's May 2026 national estimate is $1.33 to $2.71 per square foot, based on one coat. A fence is cheaper per square foot than a deck because it sees less wear, but a long run adds up, so the written quote lists the total.

How long does a staining job take?

It depends on the size of the deck or fence, the condition of the wood, and how long it needs to dry after washing. Your written quote lists a start date and the number of days, and we tell you early if repairs or weather change the schedule.

How soon can I use the deck again?

Follow the product label for the stain we use, which lists when you can walk on it and when furniture can go back. We write the stay-off time into your quote, since the stain needs to cure, not just feel dry on top.

Do you build or replace whole decks?

No. We stain decks and fences and replace damaged boards that belong to a staining job. A full deck build or rebuild is carpentry, and we will tell you if your deck needs that before you spend money on stain.
