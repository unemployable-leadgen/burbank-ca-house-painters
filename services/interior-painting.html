  • Size. More rooms, bigger rooms, and taller ceilings mean more surface to cover.

Repairs and Extras Done Before the Paint Goes On

Paint only looks as good as the surface under it, so we take care of the repairs that decide how the finished wall looks.

Drywall repair

Nail pops, dings, cracks, and water-stained patches get cut out or filled, taped, sanded smooth, and primed. Skipping the primer leaves a dull spot that shows in side light, so every patch gets sealed before the color goes on.

Wallpaper removal

Paper comes off by scoring and steaming, then the leftover adhesive gets washed off, because paint over glue turns lumpy and peels. Torn drywall paper is sealed and smoothed before primer.

Cabinet painting

Kitchen and bath cabinets get their doors removed, then are degreased, sanded, primed, and finished with a hard-wearing cabinet paint. It refreshes a dated kitchen without the cost of a remodel, and our cabinet painting page covers the process and costs.

Popcorn ceilings

A textured ceiling can be painted as it is or scraped smooth first. Before any scraping, the texture needs a test: EPA says you cannot tell by looking whether a material contains asbestos, and lists textured paint and patching compounds used on walls and ceilings among the places it can turn up.

EPA recommends sampling by a trained, accredited asbestos professional when a material is damaged or a renovation would disturb it. We ask for the lab result before any scraping starts, and if asbestos is found, that ceiling goes to an asbestos professional first and we paint afterward.

When You Need Interior Painting

Paint tells you when it is wearing out, and a repaint costs less than the repairs that pile up when you wait. Look for these signs:

  • Scuffs that will not wipe off. Hallways, kitchens, and kids' rooms wear first.
  • Fading or yellowing. Rooms with strong window light change color fastest.
  • Patch marks showing through. Old repairs that catch the light mean the sheen no longer matches.
  • Stains and peeling. These point to moisture, which needs fixing before any repaint.
  • A move, a sale, or a new tenant. More than half the city's households rent (24,887 of 43,890 occupied homes, Census ACS 2024), so turnover repaints are a big share of calls.

Why Interior Paint Peels, Streaks, and Wears Through

When interior paint fails, the paint is rarely the cause. The cause is what happened before it went on, and it is the reason prep takes so much of a professional job.

  • Dirty or greasy walls. Paint over kitchen grease or hand oils never bonds, and it peels in sheets.
  • Glossy old paint left unsanded. New paint has nothing to grip on a slick surface.
  • Unprimed patches. A patch soaks up paint differently, so it shows as a dull flat spot.
  • Moisture. Steam and leaks lift paint in bathrooms and along exterior walls, and the leak must be fixed first.
  • Thin or cheap coats. Paint stretched too far shows roller marks and wears through fast.

Left alone, a small peel spreads and the wall underneath starts needing repair instead of a repaint.

Sheen changes how a wall looks and how well it cleans. A shinier finish wipes clean more easily but shows wall flaws more, so the finish follows the room.

Interior Painting Questions, Answered

How much does interior painting cost?

NerdWallet's 2026 figures, citing Angi data, put professional interior painting at $2 to $6 per square foot, about $2,021 on average, with labor making up 75% to 95% of the job. Prep, room size, and whether ceilings and trim are included move a bid up or down. We put the total in writing before work starts.

How long does interior painting take?

It depends on how many rooms you are painting, the ceiling height, and how much patching the walls need. Your written quote lists a start date and the number of days, so you can plan around it, and we tell you early if repairs change the schedule.

Do I have to move my furniture?

Clear small items and wall hangings if you can. Larger pieces get moved to the middle of the room and wrapped, and floors are covered before any work starts, so nothing ends up with paint on it.

Can you match a color I already have?

In most cases, yes. Give us a paint chip, the original can, or a small chip cut from the wall, and we match it before starting. We can also paint a test patch so you see the color in your own light.

Is there a lead or asbestos concern in an older home?

It can be. EPA says about three-quarters of U.S. homes built before 1978 still contain some lead-based paint, and it lists textured paint and patching compounds on walls and ceilings among the places asbestos can be found. Tell us your home's age on the first call and we plan the prep, and any ceiling testing, around it.
